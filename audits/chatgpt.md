I went through the live site, the GitHub repository/source, the CSS and interaction layer, and current 2026 web/design guidance. Your portfolio is **already much more distinctive than the typical “developer portfolio”**. The main opportunity is not to make it *more futuristic*; it is to make the existing identity **more coherent, more memorable, and more content-driven**.

Your current implementation confirms that this is deliberately built around a “nebula / system / creative technologist” concept: a dark void palette, teal/lavender accents, stars, WebGL, Lottie, GSAP, Lenis, glass cards, timeline, bento layout, typewriter roles, technical labels, and a system-style navigation.

# 2. The biggest thing I would NOT do

I would **not** follow the current portfolio fashion of:

* giant 3D object
* giant bold sans-serif heading
* black background
* neon green/purple
* cursor-following blob
* scrolling text
* grain
* magnetic buttons
* excessive hover physics
* “creative developer / digital designer” terminology
* huge case-study cards

You already have enough of that vocabulary.

Current inspiration examples are increasingly showing very minimal, typographic and art-directed experiences alongside heavily interactive ones. And modern developer portfolios also commonly use the same dark background + oversized heading + bright accent + small tech tags pattern.

That means blindly following those patterns would indeed cause the exact problem you identified.

**Your site should evolve, not trend-hop.**

# 20. Performance is your biggest technical concern

This is probably the most important engineering-side recommendation.

Your site loads:

* Three.js
* GSAP
* ScrollTrigger
* Lenis
* Lottie
* multiple Lottie animations
* large image assets
* WebGL
* starfield
* multiple animation systems
* Google Fonts

and your JS continuously renders the Three.js scene.

Your code already makes thoughtful attempts at lazy-loading Three.js/Lottie and caps WebGL pixel ratio at 1.5, which is good engineering.

But the question is no longer:

> “Can it run?”

It is:

> **“Does it remain instant while all of this runs?”**

Interaction responsiveness matters throughout the page, not merely at initial load; INP is specifically designed to capture this lifecycle-wide responsiveness. A good target is ≤200 ms at the 75th percentile. ([web.dev][2])

So I'd aggressively profile:

**mobile mid-range device**

rather than just:

**developer desktop with a powerful GPU.**

Technically impressive.

But I'd classify motion into:

### Ambient

stars / extremely subtle WebGL

### Narrative

hero reveal / timeline

### Feedback

hover / click

### Navigation

scroll transitions

Then ensure you don't have several categories competing simultaneously.

Modern web guidance explicitly recommends respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`, particularly for parallax, zooming and other decorative motion. ([web.dev][3])

Your future version should therefore have something like:

**Motion: ON / Reduced**

That would actually fit your existing system aesthetic perfectly.

# 22. Make accessibility part of the aesthetic

This could be done without ruining the visual identity.

Your site already has several good semantic/accessibility details such as labels and ARIA attributes in the source.

I'd add:

`[MOTION: FULL]`

which can switch to:

`[MOTION: REDUCED]`

and perhaps:

`[CONTRAST: NORMAL]`

This sounds like a tiny detail, but it makes accessibility feel **native to the design language** rather than bolted on.

Also make sure:

* keyboard navigation is obvious
* focus states are visible
* all interactive project cards are keyboard accessible
* reduced motion genuinely stops decorative motion
* WebGL has a static fallback

Keyboard/focus order and reduced-motion handling are both important parts of a usable animated interface. ([web.dev][4])

Your portfolio already has enough visual personality to avoid the generic developer-site problem. The next step isn't more effects, more gradients, more 3D, more fonts or more trends.

It is **curation**.

Your most valuable asset is actually the weird combination of things you work on: application development, Laravel/PHP, Python, AI/NLP, FFI/native systems, ephemeris/scientific computation, linguistics, literature and curiosity about science/philosophy. Your current Work section already exposes some of that unusual mix, especially JME Ephemeris, Panchang Core, the FFI ecosystem and Poetry Analyzer. ([Jayesh Me Pani][1])

That should become the **design concept itself**.

And there is a very nice modern-web direction available for that: browsers are increasingly capable of scroll-driven animation, view transitions and rich interaction without requiring every effect to be JavaScript-heavy, while performance and responsiveness remain critical. ([Chrome for Developers][5])

So my recommendation is:

> **Keep the Nebula Noir foundation.
> Strip away ~20% of the decorative noise.
> Add ~40% more intellectual/content depth.
> Make your unusual engineering work the visual protagonist.
> Build one or two signature interactions around that work.**

That would preserve what makes your portfolio *yours* while making it significantly more polished, memorable and effective in 2026.
